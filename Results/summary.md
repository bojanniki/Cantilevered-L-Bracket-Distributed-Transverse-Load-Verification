**Summary**

This analysis checks whether the L-bracket (S235JR, 1.5 mm thick, 48 mm wide, 100 mm arms, 2 mm inside bend radius) can safely support a 10 kg (98.1 N) distributed load on its horizontal arm with a target safety factor of 1.5. An analytical hand calculation was performed and cross-checked against FEA results from Solid Edge Simulation (NX Nastran).

Both methods identify the bend — not the fixed wall — as the critical section, since the 90° corner concentrates stress more severely there than the growing bending moment along the vertical arm would suggest on its own. The hand calculation gives a peak bending stress of 272.5 MPa and a safety factor of 0.86; FEA gives 261 MPa and 0.902. The two agree within about 4%, which gives confidence that both the analytical model and the FEA setup are capturing the same governing behavior correctly.

A standard stress-concentration factor (Kt) was considered but not applied to the final result: typical notch charts assume a stepped-shoulder geometry that doesn't match this bracket's constant-thickness knee corner, and applying one would only widen the gap with FEA rather than close it. FEA is therefore treated as the more reliable source for the true local peak stress, while the hand calculation validates the overall load path and global bending behavior.

**Both methods agree: the current design fails the target safety factor of 1.5 and, in fact, exceeds the material's yield strength under the specified load.** A parametric redesign sweep shows that increasing sheet thickness from 1.5 mm to approximately 2.0 mm brings the safety factor to the target, since bending stress scales with 1/t² — making thickness the most effective single design change. Shortening the horizontal arm is a secondary option but less effective on its own. The recommended next step is to update the CAD model with the increased thickness and re-run the FEA to confirm the predicted improvement before finalizing the design.
